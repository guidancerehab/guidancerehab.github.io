What is usually going on

  • An irritated rotator cuff tendon from doing too much too soon, or from not doing enough for a long time
  • Frozen shoulder, where the joint gets tight and stiff over months, more common between 40 and 60 and with diabetes
  • Weak upper back and shoulder blade muscles making the cuff work overtime
  • Scans often show tears that are normal for your age and do not explain the pain

How I treat it

  • Find the movements that are irritated and the ones that are safe to load
  • Hands on treatment and dry needling to ease pain so you can train
  • Progressive cuff and upper back strength, tracked so you can see it improve
  • A clear path back to the gym, your sport, or just sleeping through the night

Common questions

Do I need an MRI or a shot?
Usually not to start. Most shoulder pain improves with the right strength work. If it is not progressing, I will help you decide on next steps.
How long does frozen shoulder take?
It can take months, but the right plan keeps you moving and more comfortable while it runs its course.
Can I keep lifting?
Usually yes, with changes. Stopping everything tends to make shoulders worse.
